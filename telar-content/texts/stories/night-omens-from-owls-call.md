---
title: "Owl's call"
---

According to Book 5 of the Florentine Codex, the horned owl's song was interpreted as a warning that something bad could happen. If the owl was heard hooting from the rooftop or a nearby tree, it was understood that death, sickness, or other misfortune would occur. In the Nahuatl translation, it gives more specific examples, including the death of a son, an enslaved person fleeing, or destruction of a person's home. This shows that the owl's call was not connected to a person but also threatened the person's family/household. (_Florentine Codex_, Book 5, ch.4, fol.7r)

Malgaloni Kerpal explains that the Florentine Codex connects alphabetic writing with the Mesoamerican tradition of communicating knowledge to images. The owl in this case is an image because it works with the written description to communicate the meaning of the omen. ( Magaloni Kerpal 152-153).
