The Fifth Book of the Florentine Codex uses this image to describe the following text: 
"...when two siblings were drinking, if the younger one drank first, the older sibling would say to \[the younger one\], 'Do not drink before I do, because, if you drink first, you will not grow anymore. You will remain just as you are now.'" (Digital Florentine Codex).

While this is a straightforward description, both the text and the image bring an insight into the internal family dynamics of a Nahua family.

## The Importance of the Older Siblings

The scene exemplifies the preference and status older siblings held in Nahua society. Even though Nahua society did not make use of the institution of the first-born (or primogénito) as they did in Europe, they still exhibited a preference for the older siblings in a family. This superstition could be the product of this cultural context.

## The Mother's Role, and Facial Expressions

In the middle of the scene, we find a feminine figure that can be interpreted as the mother or caretaker of the siblings. She plays an important role by being the only one standing up. With one hand, she holds what we can assume to be a container of liquid. She points at the older sibling while facing the younger. She has speaking glyphs directed to the younger sibling. Both siblings are complaining to their mother, pointing at her and having demanding expressions on their faces.

This lively scene captures how the prioritization of the older sibling was established inside Nahua homes, while also portraying a timeless feud between siblings mediated by their mother.
