---
title: "Two versions of the Omen"
---

In both versions of the text, the owl itself was seen to represent the owl as an omen. However, the description of the omen itself is not seen to be presented the same way. In the Nahuatl version, they specify the consequences more, such as sickness, death, a son dying, an exnslaved person fleeing, and the destruction of the person's home. The Spanish text includes similar ideas, however, in a broader way.

In the article, Terraciano explains that the Spanish version of the Florentine Codex does not directly translate the Nahuatl version accurately. Between the two texts, it clearly shows how the Spanish version of the summary tends to leave out parts of the Nahuatl text. Explaining why the Nahuatl description of the owl omen contains more details than the Spanish version (Terraciano 12)
